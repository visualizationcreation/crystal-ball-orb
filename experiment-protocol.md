# The fourteen-day crystal-ball experiment

Status: exploratory AI-assisted forecasting, not an AGI test. Forecast edition 2026-09-08-v1; September 9–22, 2026. World Watch is a separate, unscored scenario companion.

Freeze the eight original claims, percentages, resolution rules and sources before their events. Keep orb.json and its hash. The local hash proves file identity, not independent publication time; the public repository history records when this snapshot was first committed. Compare that timestamp with each event’s outcome time. A late publication must disclose its date and cannot serve as prospective registration for events already known then.

Resolve every call with its original rule. Add a dated source and one of YES, NO, provisional or unresolved. Leave cancellations, missing evidence and postponed results visible. Record the reason; never quietly remove a miss. Final certification can occur later where the original rule permits it. A September 23 review may therefore contain provisional results.

For each resolved binary call use Brier loss (p − y)², with p as a fraction and y = 1 for YES or 0 for NO. Lower is better. Report the mean across resolved calls, the number resolved out of eight, and every unresolved case. A fixed 50% baseline has loss 0.25 on each resolved binary call. It is only an elementary comparison; beating it on deliberately selected, often predictable events is weak evidence. A stronger follow-up should preregister matched no-change/base-rate and independent human or market forecasts before the events, without importing later odds.

These probabilities are subjective author judgments produced in an assisted workflow, not calibrated model outputs. Event selection was not randomized. Several policy calls share energy exposure; count that dependence when interpreting success. No model-only condition, blinding, repeated trials or independent human control was run. Eight correlated cases cannot demonstrate calibration, general intelligence, causal understanding or access to future information.

Keep the original score alongside any later revised probability. Compare revisions as separate dated forecasts. Score World Watch narratives only if they are converted into new, precisely defined forecasts before their outcomes; broad scenarios are not eligible retroactive hits.

The useful question: does this process help us ask better questions, find relevant evidence, express uncertainty and learn from errors? The fun question: how much of the next fortnight can a crystal-ball orb help us anticipate? The results must answer the first question before they can illuminate the second.

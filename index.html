    The fourteen-day experiment

    Curiosity, with a record.

    Could AI-assisted research help us anticipate a little more of the near future? This orb turns that question into eight dated calls you can inspect.

    The crystal ball and small UAP mark bring the playfulness. The probabilities, evidence and complete record bring the test.

    1. Freeze the calls. Preserve the September 8 edition and its rules before the events. Local file hashes identify the snapshot; they are not independent proof of its publication time.
    2. Keep every result. Record YES, NO, provisional or unresolved with a dated source. The World Watch scenarios are unscored context, not extra wins to claim later.
    3. Compare carefully. Use Brier loss, (p − outcome)², on resolved binary calls. A 50% baseline scores 0.25. Report the unresolved count too. Future rounds need matched base rates and independent comparison forecasts.
    4. Learn within the limits. Eight selected, partly correlated judgments cannot establish calibration, AGI or foreknowledge. They can show where our evidence and reasoning helped—or failed.

    How to read this crystal ball

    What is fixed: the forecast cutoff is September 8, 2026, in America/Los_Angeles. The next fourteen calendar days are September 9–22 inclusive. Event dates use the organizer’s local date. Each scoring rule states its own cutoff where needed. A cancellation or postponement beyond the window remains unresolved, rather than counting as a policy or sporting outcome.

    What the colors mean: lavender points are interpretive drivers; gold points are outcome guesses; blue points contain cited observations, assumptions, and knowledge-floor questions. Connections express a reason to investigate, not proof of causation. The lower floor marks the limits of this edition’s research, not a universal limit of knowledge.

    How to update fairly: preserve this edition and its probabilities. Gather the dated outcome source, apply the original rule, and label YES, NO, provisional, or unresolved. A later forecast should be a separate revision. Probabilities are deliberately coarse; this set has not been statistically calibrated. Selection favors scheduled events with observable outcomes and is not a comprehensive ranking of world importance.
